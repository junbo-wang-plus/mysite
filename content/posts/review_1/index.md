# [Klara and the Sun](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/54120408)
{{< byline author="Kazuo Ishiguro" year="2021" >}}

![](Klara_and_the_Sun.png)

A world where robots with the mind of a child and yet superhuman abilities are being sold as Artificial Friends (AFs) for children. I love that you could not really tell what Ishiguro's message is. Most of the story revolves around how an AF, Klara, interprets and acts in the world as she spends her time with her child companion. The choice that the AFs are fully equipped top-of-the-spec shells and yet near zero understanding of the human world makes it less a story about robots but one about humans. We grow up and move on. We disappoint each other. We accept this as the necessary human condition. But to Klara, all she had left in the end was the benevolent Sun.

	
# [Machines like Me](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/42086795)
{{< byline author="Ian McEwan" year="2019" >}}

![](MachinesLikeMe.jpg)

Another robot sold as a companion. This time the story puts more focus on alternative history in the late 90s England where AI and science have made much more progress. Adam is one of two dozens of robots newly developed and sold to humans as companions. Like Klara, Adam is learning about the human world fresh while already equipped with incomprehensible superhuman abilities. The speed at which he grows in cognition is astonishing. He learns about the world and combs through his thought in the background when he's charging. Stock market is too easy for him. He churns out haikus to express his love to his owner. He jokes about breaking his other owner’s arm if he tries to shut him off. Yet he is loyal. But to what? At the beginning, he passes perfectly fine as a housechore robot. However, cracks start to show up. He follows orders but he has his own agenda too. Eventually the gap between his and his owners' flawed morals grows to be unbridgeable so they become incomprehesible to each other. This inherent difference is darkly funny, absurd, and at times melancholic. In the end, he betrays his owners to follow his morals and they betray him in turn.
